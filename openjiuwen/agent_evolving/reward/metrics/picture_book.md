# Picture Book Quality Principles

**Purpose:** general principles for judging a delivered picture book. They say what good means, not what a particular book must contain or look like. The criteria writer turns them into task-specific, checkable claims; this document sets no counts, sizes or other fixed numbers, and never prescribes a plot, a style or a layout.

## What good means

1. **It is the book that was asked for.** The requested pages, characters, setting, theme, audience and format are there, in order, and the book opens and reads as delivered.
2. **The story moves.** Someone wants something, tries, meets resistance and changes through their own actions; each page turn brings something new; the ending answers the beginning.
3. **It is written to be read aloud to its audience.** Language fits the age; sentences have rhythm and sound; the story shows rather than lectures.
4. **Words and pictures work together.** They never contradict each other, and each does what the other cannot: the pictures carry feeling, action and detail the words leave unsaid.
5. **The world stays the same world.** Characters keep who they are and how they look across pages; places keep their layout; every named character is recognisably themselves.
6. **The pictures are well made in their own style.** Figures are whole and coherent, action and emotion are readable, and each picture has a clear point of attention. Pictures are judged against published picture books of the same style, never against fixed rules for size, framing or space.
7. **The theme lives in the story.** The central idea is carried by what happens and what is shown, consistently, and nothing in the book teaches its opposite.
8. **It is finished.** Text is legible and placed with care; the presentation is consistent from page to page; every page renders where it is delivered.

## How to judge (evidence)

- **Every page.** Claims about consistency, correctness and craft are checked on all pages; failures name their pages.
- **Failed is not unverified.** A page that could not be seen is unknown, not unmet.
- **Pictures by eye.** Visual claims are judged from the page images, as a reader would see them.
- **Concrete, not fixed.** Each claim names one observable property of this book. Taste is not turned into numbers.
- **The book's own words are not proof.** What the final reply says the book has must be visible in the book.

## Craft references (insight only)

Look for repetition the audience can join, a readable progression, pictures that carry emotion beyond the words, and a structure that serves reading. Transfer the principle, never another book's characters, plot or content.
