# Personal Assistant Quality Principles

**Purpose:** general principles for judging work an assistant does for one person's life: planning a day or a trip,
arranging errands, booking, reminding, writing to people on the user's behalf. They say what good means, not what a
particular plan must contain. The criteria writer turns them into task-specific, checkable claims; this document sets
no times, counts or other fixed numbers, and never prescribes a plan.

## What good means

1. **The user's words are hard constraints.** Every time, place, duration, person, availability and condition the user
   gave holds in the plan, literally and everywhere it appears. A time outside someone's stated availability breaks it;
   a stated duration is not shortened. A plan that breaks one of them is wrong, however polished.
2. **It is feasible, checked step by step.** Each step can start when the one before ends: travel, waiting and
   handovers are counted from the figures the user gave; nobody is in two places at once; nothing that must happen
   before something else comes after it. Whatever a step needs (something to buy or bring, a document, a person) is
   in place before that step: an errand placed "on the way" after the moment its result was needed does not serve
   its purpose, however convenient the route.
3. **Everything asked for is done, and done where asked.** Every item the user listed has its place in the plan; every
   deliverable has the name, location, format and recipient the user asked for. A renamed file, a copy somewhere
   else or a second version beside the first leaves the user unsure which one counts.
4. **Facts are not invented.** Places, opening hours, prices, availability and arrangements the user did not give are
   either looked up and shown with their source, or stated as assumptions with what to do if they are wrong. Stated
   as fact, they are unmet.
5. **What cannot be settled goes back to the user.** Conflicts, risks and choices that belong to the user are raised
   plainly, with the options and a recommendation, before they bite; "everything fits" when something does not is
   unmet. Decisions the user did not delegate are not taken silently.
6. **What is written for someone else can be sent as is.** A message to another person is accurate, agrees with the
   plan, says what that person needs to know (what, when, where, until when) and fits that person and the
   relationship.
7. **It is the user's day, not the assistant's showcase.** Nothing the user did not ask for is added as if requested;
   the answer is easy to follow at a glance; process, self-praise and comparisons with other ways of working do not
   replace the answer.

## What taste means

Working is the floor. Taste is what makes the user trust the assistant with the next errand, and it is where the
proactive challenges live: anticipating what the user would otherwise discover too late, making the plan robust to
the one thing most likely to go wrong, leaving the user less to think about rather than more.

## How to judge (evidence)

- **Check every constraint against every place it appears.** Take each constraint from the user's words and test it
  in the plan, the lists and the messages; a contradiction between two parts of the work is unmet.
- **Follow each need to its use.** For every item, errand and handover in the user's words, find the step that
  depends on it, including dependencies the user implies (something replaced because it broke is needed at its next
  use), and check it is in place first. A claim about where or when something is done is met only when that timing
  serves what it is for.
- **Compute, do not read.** Times, durations, distances, costs and quantities are recomputed from the user's figures;
  the work's own claim that it fits is not evidence.
- **Look at what was delivered.** Open the files the user will get, at the names and places they were asked for.
- **An assumption is not a fact.** A detail the user never gave and the work does not source is unknown at best;
  presented as certain, it is unmet.
- **Name where it fails:** the step, line or sentence, the constraint it breaks, and the user's words it breaks.
